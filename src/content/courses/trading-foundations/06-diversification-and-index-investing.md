## Not all your eggs in one basket

**Diversification** means holding things that do not all move for the same reasons, so one bad outcome does not sink the whole portfolio. The goal is to reduce the damage from any single holding, not to guarantee profits.

Owning ten stocks in the same industry is diversified by *name* but not by *industry*: one regulation or price shock can hit all ten. Real spread comes from different companies, industries, sizes and asset types.

## What it cannot do

Diversification does not stop **market-wide** declines. In a broad sell-off, most holdings fall together. It reduces *single-name* risk, not *market* risk.

## Index investing

An **index fund** or index ETF simply holds everything in an index, for example the largest companies, weighted by size. There is no stock picker to pay, so the **expense ratio** is usually very low. Many indexes hold hundreds of companies, so you get wide diversification in one purchase.

**Fees compound.** On $10,000 a fund charging 1.00% costs $100 a year; an index ETF at 0.05% costs $5. The difference is $95 per year, every year, taken out of your returns before they reach you. Whether any approach wins over time is debated, and none is certain. Costs are one of the few things you can see in advance.

## Keeping it balanced

Prices drift, so a winner can grow into a large share of your portfolio. **Rebalancing** means trimming what has grown too large and adding to what has lagged, to return to your plan. It is a risk-control habit, not a way to beat the market.
