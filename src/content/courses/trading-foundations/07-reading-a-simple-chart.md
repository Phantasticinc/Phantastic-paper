## What a price chart shows

A price chart plots price over time, usually as a line or as candles. It shows **what happened**, not what will happen. At best it helps you describe the current situation and plan around it.

## Trend

- **Uptrend**: a series of **higher highs and higher lows**. Each rally goes a bit further and each dip stops a bit higher.
- **Downtrend**: **lower highs and lower lows**.
- **Sideways (range)**: neither, with price bouncing between a floor and a ceiling.

Trends depend on the **timeframe**. A stock can be in a downtrend over a week and an uptrend over a year. Look at more than one timeframe before drawing conclusions.

## Support and resistance

- **Support** is a price area where falling prices have repeatedly stopped or bounced, because buyers showed up there before.
- **Resistance** is an area where rising prices have repeatedly stalled, because sellers showed up there before.

These are **zones**, not exact lines, and not guarantees. If price falls decisively below a well-tested support, the level has failed, and the idea that buyers defend it is weaker. A broken support area is often watched as new resistance.

## How people use it

A trader might wait for a pullback to support in an uptrend and place a stop just below it. If support breaks, the idea is wrong and the stop gets them out (lesson 5). That links charts to sizing (lesson 4): the chart suggests *where* to be wrong; sizing decides *how much* to risk.

## Limits

- Patterns look obvious afterwards and are ambiguous live.
- Different people draw different lines on the same chart.
- Volume and news matter, and a chart cannot see surprises.
