## The market is the easy part

Most people know "buy low, sell high". Following your own plan when money is moving is the hard part. Common traps:

- **FOMO** (fear of missing out): buying after a sharp rise because it feels urgent, often near where excitement peaks.
- **Loss aversion**: feeling losses more strongly than equal gains, so you hold a loser hoping to get back to even and cut winners too early.
- **Revenge trading**: trying to win back a loss immediately with bigger, less careful trades.
- **Overconfidence**: a few wins make you size up and ignore your rules.

## Decide before you click

The best antidote is to write the plan **before** entering: why you are taking the trade, entry, stop, target, size (lesson 4), and the condition that tells you the idea is wrong. Then your job is to follow it, not to re-decide under pressure.

## Process versus outcome

A good trade can lose and a bad trade can win. If you followed your plan and the stop got hit, the *process* was fine. Do not tear up good rules after one result. Judge yourself on whether you followed the plan across many trades.

## A simple journal

After each trade, write down:

1. **Date and symbol**
2. **Why** (the idea in one sentence)
3. **Entry / stop / target / size**
4. **How I felt** before and after (calm, anxious, excited)
5. **Result**
6. **Did I follow my plan?** If not, why not?
7. **One lesson**

Review it weekly. Patterns appear in your *decisions*, not only in your profit and loss: for example "I break my rules after two losses in a row".

## Paper trading helps, up to a point

Practice is a safe place to build habits, but fake money does not carry real fear or greed, so your behaviour with real money can differ. Treat paper results with humility. See the [risk notice](/risk).
